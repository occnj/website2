# Editing the Oasis website — a guide for staff

This is for anyone on the church team who needs to change wording, images, or
links on the website. You don't need to know anything technical. If you can use
a word processor, you can do this.

Take your time, and remember: **nothing you type goes live until you click
Publish.** You can't break anything just by looking around.

---

## 1. Sign in

1. Go to the website address followed by `/admin` (for example
   `oasischurch.org/admin`).
2. Enter your **Username or email** and **Password**, then click **Sign In**.
3. If you've forgotten your password, click **Forgot password?** and follow the
   email instructions.

If you don't have a login, ask whoever manages the site to create one for you.
Only accounts set up as an **owner, admin, or editor** are allowed to publish
changes — see "If your changes won't save" near the end if you run into trouble.

---

## 2. Two ways to edit a page

Once signed in, click **Pages** in the left-hand menu. Each page appears as a
card. Every card has two buttons at the bottom:

- **Fields** — a simple form for the structured parts of a page (the page title,
  main image, and the SEO description that shows up in Google). Use this when you
  want to change one specific, labelled thing.
- **Visual edit ↗** — opens the *real* page in a new tab with editing turned on.
  This is the one to use for changing wording anywhere on the page. It's the
  recommended way to edit text.

The green **Published** badge in the top-left corner of each card tells you the
page is live on the site. A grey **Hidden** badge means it isn't.

---

## 3. Editing text (the Visual editor)

After you click **Visual edit ↗**, the page opens with a dark bar across the top.
That bar is your control strip — it shows whether your work is saved and has the
**Publish** button.

To change wording:

1. **Move your mouse over any text.** It will highlight to show it's editable.
2. **Click it** and start typing, just like in a document. You can edit almost
   anything you can see — headings, paragraphs, buttons, even addresses and
   phone numbers.
3. **Click somewhere else** when you're done with that piece.

Other things you can do:

- **Change a picture:** click the image (or drag a photo file straight onto it)
  and choose a new one. It uploads and replaces automatically.
- **Change where a button goes:** hover the button and use the **🔗 Edit link**
  option, then type the new web address.
- **Find every bit of text at once:** use the **All text** button in the top bar.
  This opens a searchable list of all wording on the page — handy for fixing a
  phrase when you're not sure where it is, or for editing hidden text like form
  confirmation messages.

A note on forms: while you're editing, the page's forms (contact, prayer, etc.)
**can't actually be submitted** — that's on purpose, so you can safely edit their
wording. You'll see "Exit the editor to submit forms" if you try.

---

## 4. Saving your work — click Publish

Look at the top bar. It always tells you the current state:

| What it says | What it means |
|---|---|
| **All changes saved** | Nothing new to publish. |
| **Unsaved changes** | You've made edits that aren't live yet. |
| **Publishing…** | Your click is being saved. |
| **Published — live on the site** | Done! Your change is now public. |
| **Saved locally (sign in to publish live)** | See the warning below. |

**To make your edits public, click the Publish button in the top bar.** Wait for
it to say **Published — live on the site**. That's the only step that changes the
real website.

> ⚠️ **Important:** If Publish says *"Saved locally (sign in to publish live)"*
> instead of *"Published — live on the site,"* your change did **not** go public —
> it was only saved on your own computer. This usually means your login session
> expired. Sign in again at `/admin` and re-publish. (See the troubleshooting
> section.)

You don't have to finish everything in one sitting. If you close the tab with
unsaved edits, the editor keeps a private draft on your computer and offers to
restore it next time — you'll see **"Recovered unpublished draft."** But a draft
is only on that one computer until you Publish, so publish when you're happy.

---

## 5. If you see "saved edits could not be placed"

Occasionally, after the website's design has been updated, you might open a page
and see an amber message like **"2 saved edits could not be placed — click to
review."**

Here's what that means, in plain terms: an edit that was saved earlier no longer
lines up with the page because the page's layout changed. **Nothing is lost.**

1. **Click the message.** A panel opens listing each edit that couldn't be
   placed, showing you exactly what the wording was and why it didn't fit.
2. Find that spot on the page and **re-type the change**, the normal way.
3. **Publish.**

It's a rare housekeeping task, not an error you caused. If it happens a lot after
a redesign, let the site manager know.

---

## 6. A few sensible habits

- **Read before you publish.** Whatever's on screen is exactly what the public
  will see. Double-check spelling and that you edited the right spot.
- **One change at a time is fine.** You can publish after every small edit, or do
  several and publish once — either way works.
- **The editor signs you out after 5 minutes of inactivity** for security. If you
  step away and come back, you may need to sign in again. Your unpublished work is
  kept as a draft, so you won't lose it — just re-publish after signing back in.
- **When in doubt, don't publish.** Leaving something as an unpublished draft is
  completely safe. Ask for a second pair of eyes.

---

## 7. Troubleshooting

**"I clicked Publish but it says 'Saved locally'."**
Your session expired or you're not signed in as a publishing role. Go to `/admin`,
sign in again, return to the page (`Visual edit ↗`), and click Publish. If it
still won't publish live, your account may not have editor permissions — ask the
site manager to check your role.

**"My change isn't showing on the real website."**
Make sure the top bar said **Published — live on the site** (not "Saved
locally"). If it did, try refreshing the public page. If it's still not there,
note the page and what you changed, and tell the site manager.

**"I accidentally changed the wrong thing."**
If you haven't published yet, just fix it back or close the tab without
publishing. If you already published, edit it again to the correct wording and
publish once more.

**"An image won't upload."**
You'll see "Upload failed." Try a smaller file or a standard format (JPG or PNG).
If it keeps failing, tell the site manager.

**Still stuck?** Write down the page, what you were doing, and any message you
saw on screen, and pass it to whoever manages the website. That detail makes it
much easier to help you quickly.
